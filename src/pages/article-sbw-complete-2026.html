An independent artist and music community history

On July 10, 2025, an experimental synthwave track named Neon Dreams marked the beginning of SynthBeatsWaves (SBW), an independent music community founded and managed by XALPHYN.

What began with one track gradually evolved into an extensive collection of AI-assisted musical experiments, alternative versions, cinematic visual presentations, and creative collaborations across different genres.

By November 13, 2026, the public historical catalog contained 17 distinct titles and 27 published versions.

Behind those releases, however, was a complicated journey: music created through accessible tools, videos produced almost entirely on a smartphone, periods of unexpected popularity, long gaps between uploads, incomplete platform catalogs, and an ongoing effort to organize everything properly.

This article documents that history, explains the current condition of SBW, and introduces XALPHYN's plans for the future.

01. Who is XALPHYN?

XALPHYN is an independent synthwave artist and experimental digital identity associated with electronic music, creative exploration, and online publishing.

Although synthwave is closely associated with XALPHYN, the identity is not restricted to a particular musical genre.

An essential distinction must be understood: XALPHYN was established primarily as an experiment in creativity, learning, and developing a recognizable digital footprint. It was not originally intended to become a conventional professional music career.

The individual behind XALPHYN has a personal life, responsibilities, interests, and priorities that exist independently of this public digital identity. These should not be treated as identical to XALPHYN's public projects.

Consequently, the identity cannot always maintain a continuous publishing schedule. Music and content creation remain activities pursued when time and circumstances permit.

The public philosophy associated with the identity can be expressed through the phrase "My existence is my silent era." It represents a quieter approach to developing a digital presence: exploration and progress do not always need constant public attention.

02. What is SynthBeatsWaves?

SynthBeatsWaves, abbreviated as SBW, is a multi-genre music community and independent presenting-label identity founded by XALPHYN on July 10, 2025.

Its function is to organize, publish, and share music experiments with listeners and digital content creators.

XALPHYN is the artist. SynthBeatsWaves is the presenting community and label identity.

This distinction is especially important because some earlier publications credited SBW as the artist. The present structure separates the identity of the artist from the community that presents the work.

SBW is not described here as a traditional commercial record company or established music-distribution business. Its purpose remains primarily community-oriented and experimental.

03. The meaning of Synth, Beats, and Waves

The name SynthBeatsWaves combines three complementary ideas.

Synth represents synthesizers, electronic sound, and especially synthwave, the genre that originally inspired SBW.

Beats represents rhythmic energy and the numerous genres built around memorable percussion, driving bass, and musical movement. This includes EDM, phonk, dance-oriented pop, and other rhythm-driven styles.

Waves represents musical atmosphere, emotion, and the distinctive listening experiences that different songs create: relaxation, nostalgia, excitement, melancholy, or uplifting energy.

Together, these concepts establish a broader musical identity than synthwave alone.

SynthBeatsWaves was designed to accommodate different musical tastes rather than serve only one audience or genre.

04. July 10, 2025 — The first release

The first public track associated with SynthBeatsWaves was Neon Dreams, released on July 10, 2025.

It established the original synthwave direction and marked the beginning of XALPHYN's music-related publishing experiments under the SBW identity.

The project began modestly, using accessible tools and mobile technology rather than a professional production environment.

A second version of Neon Dreams also appeared, introducing a pattern that continued through much of the early catalog: publishing alternative interpretations of the same song.

On July 10, 2026, SBW completed its first anniversary. As of November 13, 2026, the community had existed for more than sixteen months.

That period included musical experimentation, audience growth, collaborative content, and major challenges involving publishing consistency and managing a growing archive.

05. The expansion beyond synthwave

Following Neon Dreams, XALPHYN explored additional musical directions rather than remaining exclusively focused on synthwave.

Beat The Odds introduced energetic electronic pop. Memory Reboot explored darker bass-heavy styles associated with trap and Brazilian phonk influences.

Tracks such as Neon Drive, Infinity Drive, and Midnight Journey expanded the electronic and EDM catalog.

Neon HeartBeat Fade presented a more relaxing atmosphere, while Neon Pulse combined synthwave with automotive-themed visual content.

Noche Salvaje and Bajo La Luna introduced Spanish-language reggaeton influences. Cara Dura! explored another intense, bass-driven direction.

These releases gradually transformed the project from a small synthwave experiment into a multi-genre music community.

Musical diversity remains one of SBW's defining objectives.

06. How the original release system worked

Throughout the project's early development, YouTube served as the primary platform for releasing music.

When XALPHYN publicly uploaded a music video for a track to the SBW YouTube channel, that publication was regarded as the track's release within the community.

This was different from formal distribution to commercial music streaming services.

A track did not need to appear on Spotify or Apple Music before being considered publicly available through SBW.

The approach was accessible and practical, but it created one major dependency.

Every completed audio track needed a video presentation before it could be published through the established workflow.

This dependency eventually became a significant cause of delays.

07. Why the early catalog included second versions

Many early SBW tracks were released in two versions.

These versions generally represented different musical interpretations of the same underlying composition.

The arrangement, performance, vocal delivery, or production characteristics might change while the song remained recognizably related to its original version.

When a track included lyrics, the second version generally preserved the words while changing aspects of their musical presentation.

This allowed XALPHYN to explore different possibilities without treating every alternative interpretation as a completely unrelated song.

The historical catalog therefore contains 17 distinct track titles and 27 individual versions.

08. October 2025 — Galactic Dreams and reduced activity

An important turning point followed the release of Galactic Dreams in October 2025.

The track was a relatively lengthy emotional English-language song, representing another development in SBW's musical range.

Around this stage, publishing consistency began declining.

New releases became less frequent. The periods between uploads grew longer, and eventually the community experienced extended periods of inactivity.

The reduced publishing activity did not necessarily reflect a lack of musical ideas.

Instead, it arose from a combination of limited time, increasing video-production demands, and responsibilities outside the XALPHYN project.

As visual presentation became more ambitious, creating and publishing each new track became more demanding.

09. The cinematic music-video era

Following the earlier period of musical experimentation, selected SBW releases began using cinematic visual concepts inspired by science-fiction films.

Rise of Tron was inspired by the futuristic atmosphere of the Tron franchise and anticipation surrounding Tron: Ares.

Tronverse continued exploring related futuristic digital-world imagery through synthwave.

Neon Hearts incorporated visual inspiration and footage associated with Blade Runner 2049.

OASIS was inspired by the virtual-world setting and concepts of Ready Player One.

These presentations represented a substantial change from earlier videos built around wallpapers and basic atmospheric effects.

However, preparing cinematic footage, synchronizing scenes, and completing detailed edits required additional time.

The film-inspired releases were independent experiments and were not official productions of, or collaborations with, the respective film studios.

Any copyrighted film footage remains subject to the rights of its owners and is not automatically available for reuse.

10. Rise of Tron — A memorable breakthrough

Among SBW's releases, Rise of Tron became a significant milestone in audience attention.

According to XALPHYN's account of the channel's performance, the music video attracted considerably more views than many earlier uploads.

It also continued receiving attention during periods when the channel had not published anything new.

This demonstrated that previously published material could continue finding listeners even without a constant stream of new releases.

The increased attention also benefited the visibility of subsequent music videos.

Within the project's history, Rise of Tron remains a particularly important release because of its audience response and the cinematic direction it represented.

11. OASIS and the effort to keep SBW active

Following the cinematic experiments and increasingly irregular publishing schedule, XALPHYN released OASIS.

The track was inspired by the film Ready Player One and its virtual-world atmosphere.

The release also represented an attempt to maintain the channel's public presence during a period when the time available for SBW had become severely limited.

Rather than representing a complete recovery of the publication schedule, OASIS showed that XALPHYN remained interested in continuing the project whenever possible.

12. Dreamy Souls — A collaboration with Metsu

On July 18, 2026, XALPHYN released Dreamy Souls.

The track was accompanied by a collaborative music video featuring visual work by Metsu (@MetsuVR).

Metsu is a supporter of XALPHYN and a member of the SBW community who creates short-form content, including music edits using VRChat gameplay and virtual environments.

The collaboration demonstrated how another creative contributor could help give an SBW track its own visual identity.

It also represented a notable return to publishing following a prolonged period of reduced activity.

As of November 13, 2026, Dreamy Souls remained the most recent publicly released track identified in the historical catalog.

The collaboration showed that SBW could provide opportunities for creative participation beyond XALPHYN's individual work.

14. Why Instagram never received every track

Although YouTube served as SBW's central release archive, the Instagram profile did not receive every corresponding music video.

One important reason was XALPHYN's dissatisfaction with the visual quality of certain older videos.

Some of those videos had been created using simple wallpapers, rainfall effects, and relatively limited editing techniques.

They were sufficient to make the tracks available on YouTube, but XALPHYN hoped to produce better presentations before publishing them on Instagram.

The desired improvements were repeatedly delayed by limited time and available resources.

As a result, the Instagram archive remained incomplete even though the corresponding tracks had already been publicly released elsewhere.

The current restoration effort is intended to address this difference without pretending that older uploads are new songs.

15. Correcting artist credits on Threads

The community's Threads presence also developed inconsistencies.

Certain earlier publications treated SynthBeatsWaves as the artist rather than identifying XALPHYN as the artist and SBW as the presenting community.

As this relationship became more clearly defined, XALPHYN began revising the way tracks were credited.

Older Threads posts cannot always be edited directly after their permitted editing window.

For that reason, corrected posts or new publications linking to the appropriate Instagram videos may be necessary.

The objective is to preserve accurate and consistent public information, not simply to increase the number of posts.

The artist is XALPHYN. The presenting community is SBW.

16. Rebuilding Audiomack

Audiomack represents another important part of the organizational process.

Some earlier tracks were uploaded under an SBW-associated artist identity.

Those uploads were later removed because the attribution no longer matched the intended separation between XALPHYN and the SynthBeatsWaves community.

XALPHYN intends to restore the audio catalog under the correct artist profile.

However, the original audio files first need to be collected, recovered, and organized.

Some files remain associated with older devices or storage locations and are not presently available on the phone used to manage the project.

Recovering the best-quality originals is preferable to relying on audio extracted from already compressed videos.

Audiomack is intended to become an additional listening destination once that recovery work is complete.

17. November 2026 — The catalog restoration initiative

During November 2026, XALPHYN outlined a plan to restore consistency across the SBW catalog.

The first stage focuses on publishing the missing historical music videos on Instagram.

The intended approach is gradual: approximately one or two videos per day, including alternative versions where appropriate.

The missing files have been gathered in preparation for this work, and scheduling is intended to make the process less demanding.

Threads will also require corrected descriptions and proper artist attribution.

Audiomack restoration will follow as the relevant original audio files are recovered.

A November 2026 Instagram Story announcement was also part of the community's communication about these changes.

The older videos are historical releases becoming available on additional platforms, rather than entirely new tracks.

The aim is to complete the major outstanding tasks by the end of 2026, subject to practical limitations.

18. The real bottleneck: visual content

XALPHYN has additional tracks prepared or awaiting release.

The central limitation is not necessarily the amount of available music.

It is the preparation of corresponding visual content.

Since YouTube became SBW's principal release platform, a completed audio track often remained unpublished until its visual presentation was ready.

Creating detailed music videos requires time, appropriate materials, editing work, and software capabilities.

Those requirements do not always fit comfortably alongside XALPHYN's other responsibilities.

The current challenge is therefore to develop a publishing method that allows music to be released consistently without requiring complex cinematic editing for every track.

19. A new approach: official audio visualizers

XALPHYN has recently explored mobile applications that produce more polished audio-reactive visualizers.

These tools make it easier to create video content without the extensive manual editing required by traditional cinematic music videos.

Future tracks may therefore be presented primarily through official music audio visualizers.

The current plan includes a higher-resolution main visualizer where available, accompanied by optional slowed, over-slowed, and slowed-and-reverb versions.

The main visual presentation may use Full HD output, while certain alternate versions may be limited to 720p because of the features available in free mobile applications.

These resolution differences are practical production choices.

Traditional music videos are not being permanently abandoned.

More elaborate presentations may still appear when time, resources, and suitable creative concepts are available.

Visualizers simply provide a more sustainable way to keep music releases moving.

20. Built and managed primarily through a phone

One of the distinctive features of SynthBeatsWaves is the role of mobile technology in its development.

From the earliest releases through the current period, XALPHYN has managed music-related content preparation, video editing, descriptions, social publishing, and community activity primarily through a smartphone.

Although a laptop was available for a limited period, it was not dedicated to producing or managing SBW's music releases.

The project continued relying on phone-based tools.

This has introduced limitations involving storage, file recovery, resolution, export settings, and editing software.

Nevertheless, XALPHYN has continued exploring practical solutions and improving the production workflow.

The ability to maintain a growing creative catalog through a phone remains a point of satisfaction for the project.

21. Artificial intelligence and creative experimentation

The music published through SBW so far has been created with assistance from AI-powered music tools.

These tools allow experimentation with musical genres, arrangements, vocal styles, rhythms, and other aspects of music production using accessible technology.

XALPHYN does not intend to present the existing catalog as entirely human-composed music.

Instead, it is understood as part of an experimental creative journey.

At the same time, XALPHYN respects the work of musicians who compose, arrange, perform, and produce music directly.

Independent music production through conventional digital audio software remains a future interest.

XALPHYN has chosen not to commercially distribute the current AI-assisted catalog through services such as Spotify.

This is a personal publishing decision, not a general assertion that AI-assisted music cannot legally be distributed.

Formal distribution is something XALPHYN would prefer to reserve for music produced through a more directly independent creative process.

22. Music for content creators — Intended reuse policy

SynthBeatsWaves aims to make eligible music available for use in digital content creation without charging a music-use fee.

XALPHYN welcomes creators who wish to use permitted tracks as background music or accompaniment in their videos, edits, and other online projects.

The intention is to make this material accessible, including through more convenient audio download options in the future.

Permission to use a track does not mean permission to claim ownership of it.

Creators should not falsely represent SBW music as their own original composition, claim exclusive rights over it, or register a recording in a copyright-claiming system as though they control it exclusively.

Attribution is appreciated:

Music: XALPHYN — [Track Title]

Presented by: SynthBeatsWaves (SBW)

Because the tracks involve AI music-generation tools, the exact rights available for reuse depend on the relevant service licenses and any third-party rights.

XALPHYN can authorize reuse only to the extent permitted by those rights.

Similarly, permission to use an audio recording does not automatically grant rights over copyrighted film footage present in an accompanying video.

The community intends to support creator-friendly use, but cannot guarantee that third-party systems will never issue automated copyright claims.

A clearer track-by-track permission system is an important direction for future development.

23. Making the music easier to access

Historically, listeners who wanted the audio file of a track often contacted XALPHYN directly.

Others obtained audio from publicly available music videos using download or extraction tools.

This was not a particularly convenient or organized way to access the catalog.

XALPHYN is now considering more accessible methods of sharing permitted audio files.

Restoring Audiomack is one immediate objective.

Another possibility is a dedicated SynthBeatsWaves website featuring track information, listening options, genre classifications, artist attribution, and downloadable audio where the rights allow it.

A dedicated application is also a longer-term idea.

Neither the website nor the application should be considered a completed or guaranteed future product.

Both remain possibilities that XALPHYN may explore after resolving the existing catalog issues.

The ultimate goal is to make music easier for listeners and creators to discover and access.

24. Additional tracks are coming

XALPHYN has more musical material prepared and awaiting public presentation.

These upcoming tracks are intended to be published under the independent SynthBeatsWaves label identity.

Although synthwave remains important to the community, the next stage is expected to give more attention to other genres.

The intention is to provide a multi-genre listening experience rather than restrict SBW to one style.

New songs may be accompanied by official visualizers and alternative slowed or reverb editions.

More complex music videos may be prepared separately when practical.

Once the organizational work is complete, XALPHYN intends to coordinate releases more consistently across the community's selected platforms.

25. The goal for the end of 2026

XALPHYN's current objective is to bring the SynthBeatsWaves publishing archive into a more stable and manageable condition before 2027.

This includes completing missing Instagram uploads, correcting Threads attribution, recovering audio files, and rebuilding the Audiomack catalog.

The objective is not to increase the burden of managing an experimental identity.

It is to reduce it.

A properly organized archive and repeatable release process should allow XALPHYN to continue publishing without repeatedly returning to old problems.

This would also free time for personal responsibilities, learning, and other activities outside the identity.

The end-of-year target remains an intention rather than an unconditional promise.

Progress may be gradual, but the direction is clear.

26. A message to listeners and supporters

SynthBeatsWaves appreciates the people who have listened to its music, followed its developments, shared its work, or offered creative support.

The community recognizes that its publishing history has not always been consistent.

Some releases were delayed. Some older videos never reached Instagram. Certain posts required credit corrections, and the Audiomack catalog needs rebuilding.

XALPHYN acknowledges these difficulties and intends to improve the process.

The community also appreciates creative contributions such as Metsu's work on Dreamy Souls.

Listeners may encounter older videos being published on Instagram or corrected information appearing on Threads during the restoration effort.

These updates are part of preserving the project's history and preparing for future releases.

There are additional tracks waiting to be shared.

27. Conclusion — The journey continues

SynthBeatsWaves began on July 10, 2025, with a single synthwave release: Neon Dreams.

Over the following months, it developed into a growing multi-genre catalog incorporating electronic pop, EDM, phonk, reggaeton, emotional songs, and cinematic synthwave.

It introduced alternative versions, visually ambitious experiments, a notable audience milestone with Rise of Tron, and a creative collaboration through Dreamy Souls.

It also encountered difficulties involving video production, time, original audio files, artist attribution, and cross-platform publishing.

By November 2026, XALPHYN had begun addressing these issues and preparing a more practical workflow for future releases.

The project remains experimental rather than a conventional full-time music career.

Its purpose is to explore, learn, create, and share musical experiences.

XALPHYN is the independent artist and experimental digital identity. SynthBeatsWaves is the community and presenting label.

More music is planned, new genres are being explored, and better ways of accessing the catalog are being considered.

More music. More genres. More experiments. More possibilities.

The journey continues.
